# Editorial terminology and localization

Use this reference when names, ambiguous terms, or localized vocabulary affect
public copy. The project owns its glossary and voice; this reference explains
how to apply them without inventing a new product vocabulary.

## Find the terminology owner

Read the affected page and any established glossary, brand guidance, or factual
source. A literal interface label takes precedence when referring to that
control. Elsewhere, prefer the clearest natural wording for the audience.

Use one consistent term per concept. Do not impose a software glossary on a
service, venue, institution, or other unrelated business. Include implementation
vocabulary only when it helps the reader understand an actual decision.

## Names and translations

- Preserve sourced proper names unless an official localized form is available.
- Localize descriptive editorial names naturally, preserving their meaning.
- Keep an unconfirmed name in its sourced form rather than inventing a commercial
  translation.
- Explain unfamiliar terms at their first relevant use, without repeating the
  explanation throughout the page.
- Choose idiomatic target-language vocabulary according to audience and context;
  an English source does not make an English technical term mandatory.

## Review localized content

Compare the affected existing language versions for factual promises,
limitations, names, and CTA destinations. They should describe the same offering
without requiring word-for-word equivalence. Follow the user's requested
language scope; do not create additional locales as an incidental copy change.

Avoid blind repository-wide substitutions. The same word can describe different
objects, literal controls, historical content, or code. Change the owning
passage after confirming its meaning and review its consumers.
