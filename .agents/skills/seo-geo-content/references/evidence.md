# Evidence for public content

Read this reference for external claims, metrics, or comparisons. Recheck
volatile sources when the content is produced.

## Use evidence that proves the claim

For business, product, or service facts, find the project's owning source and
check that it supports the claim. For external claims, prefer:

1. Current official documentation for a platform's behavior or a competitor's
   own public capabilities.
2. Original research or data, including its method and limitations.
3. Reputable independent analysis when primary evidence cannot answer the
   question.

Search results, AI answers, summaries, and generated drafts may reveal a
question or lead to a source. They prove only the observation they directly
show, not a general ranking mechanism or durable fact.

For each material claim, verify the exact proposition, source, date, scope, and
limitations. Give numbers a definition, period, and denominator. Attribute
independent observations, use quotes only with a named source and permission,
label hypotheses, and escalate conflicting or high-impact evidence. Never turn
correlation or one platform's behavior into a universal rule.

Comparisons require current evidence for every option and the same decision
criteria throughout. Distinguish what each company states from what the author
concludes, and acknowledge differences that could change the reader's choice.

## Current content principles

- Google treats generative search as an extension of SEO. It recommends
  useful, original, expert-led content and rejects separate AI-targeted copy,
  artificial chunking, fixed lengths, fake freshness, and near-duplicate pages
  for query variations. See its
  [generative AI guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
  and [people-first guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).
- Microsoft recommends intent-led, precise content with descriptive headings
  and self-contained passages, using lists, steps, Q&A, or tables when the
  reader's task benefits from them. Treat this as platform guidance, not a
  selection guarantee. See its
  [AI-search content guidance](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/october-2025/optimizing-your-content-for-inclusion-in-ai-search-answers).
- The KDD 2024 GEO study supports testing how sourcing and presentation affect
  generated answers within a defined retrieval setup. It does not establish a
  universal discovery, ranking, citation, traffic, or conversion formula. See
  [GEO: Generative Engine Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735).

Use these principles as boundaries, not claims to publish in website copy.
