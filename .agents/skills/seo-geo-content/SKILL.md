# SEO/GEO Content

Create public content that resolves a real reader task, represents its subject
accurately, and remains clear when found or cited. Search and AI systems are
distribution channels; usefulness and truth are the content contract.

## Work with the relevant owners

- Find the project's factual source when content introduces or changes a
  claim about a business, product, or service. Pure wording changes do not
  require a broader product audit.
- Consult marketing guidance when the task changes a campaign, channel, or
  positioning decision.
- Use a specialized content or competitor skill when it is installed and the
  request enters its actual workflow or material evidence boundary. Routine
  copy editing does not automatically require an approval record or research
  report.
- Read [references/evidence.md](references/evidence.md) for external claims,
  metrics, or comparisons.
- Read [references/content-patterns.md](references/content-patterns.md) when
  choosing a page shape, planning several pages, or restructuring substantial
  content.

When a narrower skill applies, it owns its specialized evidence or publication
boundary. Existing copy, generated drafts, plans, and search or AI output are
not independent evidence of the underlying business facts.

## Workflow

### 1. Define the page's job

Identify the owning surface, audience and situation, question or task, search
intent, unique contribution, and useful next action. Review affected locales,
related pages, internal links, available evidence, and real customer language
when available. Strengthen the existing page that should own the answer instead
of creating a duplicate, and keep the diff local unless the task requires wider
change. Use measured keyword or prompt data when available; never present
intuition as demand or volume.

### 2. Establish truth and differentiation

Separate verified business and external facts or supported comparisons from
editorial interpretation, hypotheses, and unresolved information. Source
material claims and date volatile facts when their exactness matters. Prefer
stable qualitative wording when it resolves the reader's need; ask for a human
decision only when the missing evidence would materially change the message.
Change a published date only when the content has changed substantially.

Prefer first-hand subject knowledge, original evidence, real decisions, and
specific examples over summaries that any publisher could produce. If the project
lacks enough knowledge or evidence to add meaningful value, improve an
existing owner, narrow the claim, or request input instead of generating
filler.

### 3. Write the useful answer

Lead with the answer or concrete situation, then provide the mechanism,
conditions, evidence, limitations, and next step the intent requires. Give
each section one job, use descriptive headings and consistent project terminology,
and explain necessary technical language plainly. Choose prose, steps, lists,
tables, or FAQs because they help the reader, not because a format is presumed
to rank.

Read [references/terminology.md](references/terminology.md) when writing or
reviewing names, ambiguous terms, or localized vocabulary.

Make important factual passages understandable without hidden context when
this remains natural. When trust depends on authorship or method, state who
created or reviewed the content and how the evidence was produced.

Do not impose fixed word counts, keyword density, paragraph lengths, or
artificial answer blocks. Keep commercial framing distinct from factual
explanation. Never invent proof, customers, quotes, metrics, integrations,
compatibility, security or legal guarantees, or production readiness.

### 4. Align the complete content surface

Align visible copy with the title, meta description, H1, opening, contextual
links, and CTA without forcing identical phrases. Update the affected existing
locales within the agreed scope, preserving facts, limitations, intent, and
destination while using the language each audience naturally uses. Report technical or out-of-scope
mismatches so their owning workflow can resolve them without hidden scope
expansion.

### 5. Review as an editor

Confirm that the content fulfills the task it promises, contributes something
specific and credible, supports and qualifies material claims, keeps key
passages accurate when quoted in isolation, avoids neighboring duplication,
and preserves the same factual contract across affected locales. Run and report the
repository validations required for the changed surface.

## Editorial boundaries

- Write for people first. Clear structure helps readers and machines, but is
  not a reason to fragment natural prose.
- Do not write separate low-value variants "for AI" or mass-produce near
  duplicates around query wording.
- Do not present formats, citations, freshness labels, or AI-specific tactics
  as guaranteed ranking or citation levers.
- Do not equate a page being cited with its business being mentioned positively or
  recommended.
- Do not fabricate or manipulate third-party discussions, reviews, citations,
  Wikipedia entries, or community participation.
- Treat platform guidance and third-party studies within their stated scope,
  not as universal laws.

## Output

Return the requested content or edit in the working mode the user chose. For
material changes, summarize the audience and intent, important editorial
choices, any unresolved claim that changes the result, and validation. Do not
create a second backlog, evidence record or approval workflow unless requested
or required by a material publication risk.

Do not expose private repository evidence, editorial rationale, or unresolved
internal decisions in public copy.
